Petition Review Flags

A check step for Indiana protection order petitions. Petitioners fill in four short pages and see gentle notes before they sign. Court staff see each petition in the order it arrived, with its flags.

    Page 1 of 4

    Why you are filing

    Tell the court what your petition is based on. Example answers are filled in so you can try the form.

    What is this petition based on?

    How to fill in this page

    1. Read the four choices and the short description under each.
    2. Pick the one that best matches what happened to you.
    3. If more than one fits, pick the main one. You will describe everything that happened on the next page.

    These descriptions are plain-language summaries, not legal definitions. If you are unsure, pick the closest one. You can come back and change it before you sign.

    Page 2 of 4

    What happened

    Describe each incident in its own box, so the judge can see when and where each thing happened.

    How to fill in this page

    1. One incident per box. If something happened on different days, use "+ Add another incident" for each day.
    2. Date: the day it happened. If you don't know the exact day, pick the closest date you remember.
    3. Place: where it happened. A general description is fine, like "outside my work" or "by text message".
    4. What happened: in your own words, what the person did or said. Include who else was there, if anyone.
    5. Photos or files (optional): for example photos of injuries or damage, or screenshots of messages.

    Leave out phone numbers and home addresses. The other party can see this part of the petition; contact details go in the confidential section.

    Page 3 of 4

    Safety questions

    Court staff see your answers exactly as you give them. They help staff see which petitions to look at first. They don't affect whether your petition is accepted.

    If you are in danger right now, call 911.

    How to fill in this page

    1. Answer each question with Yes, No or Unsure.
    2. "Unsure" is a fine answer. Choose it if you don't know.
    3. Answer based on what you know today.

    A "Yes" marks your petition for prompt review by court staff. It does not slow your petition down or count against you.

    Page 4 of 4

    Review and sign

    Check your answers before you sign. The notes below are suggestions, not requirements. You can file either way.

    This tool doesn't give legal advice or tell you whether you will get an order.

    How to use this page

    1. Read each note. Use "Edit" to go back and change something.
    2. Check the summary of your answers.
    3. When you are ready, select Sign and file.

    What the colors mean

    Yellow: privacy. The other party can see this. Blue: something may be missing. Green: nothing to check.

    Incoming petitions

    Listed in the order received. Flags never reorder or hide a petition. Select a row to see the details.
